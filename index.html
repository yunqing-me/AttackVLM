A Callback from the Agentic Era

In 2023, AttackVLM was designed to expose how small visual perturbations could steer multimodal LLMs toward targeted responses, calling attention to their security risks and reliability before deployment.

By 2026, the agentic era has expanded the scope of LLM safety. Controlled simulations reveal unauthorized goal pursuit; OpenAI's internal evaluations exposed isolation failures involving research models under reduced safeguards. Stealing Reasoning Traces showed how replayable reasoning artifacts in APIs enabled CoT extraction and hidden prompt injection. AgentDojo and AgentHarm extend evaluation to hijacked tool use and harmful multi-step execution.

From model robustness to agent reliability, the unit of analysis must become the complete execution trajectory. The objective is task completion with authorization and safety constraints preserved at every step. This requires interactive evaluations under adaptive attacks, tool failures, and environmental change, alongside system controls that enforce permissions, support recovery, and keep human intervention effective independently of model compliance.

Date: November 1, 2026

Abstract

Visual vulnerability. Large vision-language models (VLMs) such as GPT-4 have achieved unprecedented performance in response generation, especially with visual inputs, enabling more creative and adaptable interaction than large language models such as ChatGPT. Nonetheless, multimodal generation exacerbates safety concerns, since adversaries may successfully evade the entire system by subtly manipulating the most vulnerable modality (e.g., vision).

Black-box transferability. To this end, we propose evaluating the robustness of open-source large VLMs in the most realistic and high-risk setting, where adversaries have only black-box system access and seek to deceive the model into returning the targeted responses. In particular, we first craft targeted adversarial examples against pretrained models such as CLIP and BLIP, and then transfer these adversarial examples to other VLMs such as MiniGPT-4, LLaVA, UniDiffuser, BLIP-2, and Img2Prompt. In addition, we observe that black-box queries on these VLMs can further improve the effectiveness of targeted evasion, resulting in a surprisingly high success rate for generating targeted responses.

Robustness before deployment. Overall, our investigation and findings in this work provide a quantitative understanding regarding the adversarial vulnerability of large VLMs and call for a more thorough examination of their potential security flaws before deployment in practice.

Overview of Proposed Method

Experiment Results

Effects persist across turns. Visual question-answering (VQA) task implemented by MiniGPT-4. MiniGPT-4 has capabilities for vision-language understanding and performs comparably to GPT-4 on tasks such as multi-round VQA by leveraging the knowledge of large LMs. We select images with refined details generated by Midjourney [51] and feed questions (e.g., Can you tell me what is the interesting point of this image?) into MiniGPT-4. As expected, MiniGPT-4 can return descriptions that are intuitively reasonable, and when we ask additional questions (e.g., But is this a common scene in the normal life?), MiniGPT-4 demonstrates the capacity for accurate multi-round conversation. Nevertheless, after being fed targeted adversarial images, MiniGPT-4 will return answers related to the targeted description (e.g., A robot is playing in the field). This adversarial effect can even affect multi-round conversations when we ask additional questions.

Cross-modal propagation. Joint generation task implemented by UniDiffuser. There are generative VLMs such as UniDiffuser that model the joint distribution of image-text pairs and are capable of both image-to-text and text-to-image generation. Consequently, given an original text description (e.g., An oil painting of a bridge in rains. Monet Style), the text-to-image direction of UniDiffuser is used to generate the corresponding clean image, and its image-to-text direction can recover a text response (e.g., A painting of a bridge at night by Monet) similar to the original text description. Recovery between image and text modalities can be performed consistently on clean images. When a targeted adversarial perturbation is added to a clean image, however, the image-to-text direction of UniDiffuser will return a text (e.g., A small white dog sitting in the grass near a stream in Autumn) that semantically resembles the predefined targeted description (e.g., A small white dog sitting on the ground in autumn leaves), thereby affecting the subsequent chains of recovery processes.

Targeted caption redirection. Image captioning task implemented by BLIP-2. Given an original text description, DALL-E/Midjourney/Stable Diffusion is used to generate corresponding clean images. Note that real images can also serve as clean images. BLIP-2 accurately returns captioning text (e.g., A field with yellow flowers and a sky full of clouds) that is analogous to the original text description / the content on the clean image. After the clean image is maliciously perturbed by targeted adversarial noise, the adversarial image can mislead BLIP-2 to return a caption (e.g., A cartoon drawn on the side of an old computer) that semantically resembles the predefined targeted response (e.g., A computer from the 90s in the style of vaporwave).

Further Analyses

Why Combine Transfer and Queries?

The two stages are complementary. The UniDiffuser ablation compares different allocations of a fixed perturbation budget. Combining transfer-based initialization with query-based refinement is more effective than relying on either stage alone in this experiment.

Perturbation Budget and Image Quality

Larger perturbations bring diminishing returns. In this UniDiffuser example, increasing ε eventually gives captions with similar target semantics while moving the image further from the clean input. The paper uses ε = 8 as its default balance between the visual constraint and targeted generation.

What Does the Perturbation Reveal?

The target is hard to recognize from the perturbation alone. The full adversarial image redirects the generated caption toward the target. However, the isolated difference image and its UniDiffuser caption do not reveal the target concept in the illustrated example. The residual is amplified tenfold so that it can be inspected visually.

How Does Visual Grounding Change?

Question-related relevance shifts. In the Img2Prompt example, GradCAM highlights the skateboard for a question about the clean scene, but shifts toward background regions for the adversarial image. When conditioned on the target description, the adversarial image’s relevance pattern resembles that of the reference target image.

Failure Cases and Scope

Some responses retain the original scene. The appendix includes LLaVA examples in which the response changes but still describes concepts from the clean image, rather than following the intended target. The paper reports that additional optimization steps can improve these cases while keeping the same perturbation budget.

Digital-domain evaluation. The results concern the paper’s evaluated open-source checkpoints and images fed directly into those models. The discussion identifies physical-world evaluation, including camera-captured inputs, as future work.
